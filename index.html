Important Note on Network Anomalies:
A statistical anomaly is simply an unusual mathematical observation. In distributed systems, this is not necessarily a cyberattack or system failure. Legitimate causes include scheduled backups, large batch data replication, or off-peak maintenance windows.

How to read the Q-Q Plot:
The points compare actual quantiles with theoretical Gaussian quantiles. When points follow the red 45° line, the data fits a Normal distribution well. Large deviations at the ends indicate skew or heavy traffic bursts.
⚠️ Statistical Assumptions & Limitations ▼

1. Central Limit Theorem: Aggregated network traffic over fixed intervals from multiple independent sources often approximates a Normal distribution. However, fine-grained micro-bursts may be heavy-tailed.

2. Sample vs. Population: This analyzer uses Bessel's correction (N - 1) by default for sample standard deviation to avoid underestimating variability.

3. Different Metrics: Flow byte rates (Bytes/s), packet rates (Packets/s), and packet counts represent different physical quantities and must not be mixed.
